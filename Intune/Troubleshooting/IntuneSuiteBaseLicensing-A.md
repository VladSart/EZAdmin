## Scope & Assumptions

This runbook covers the **licensing/entitlement mechanics** of Microsoft folding a substantial slice of the paid Intune Suite add-on into base Microsoft 365 E3 and E5 licensing, effective **1 July 2026** with rollout completing by **1 August 2026**. It does not cover the technical deployment or troubleshooting of any individual bundled capability — those live in their own dedicated runbooks (`EPM-A/B.md`, `RemoteHelp-A/B.md`, `CloudPKI-A/B.md`, `EnterpriseAppManagement-A/B.md`). This is deliberately scoped as a licensing/procurement topic because, in practice, the vast majority of MSP-facing tickets generated by this change are about entitlement confusion (unexpected access, suspected double-billing, tier mismatches) rather than a technical fault in any one feature.

Assumes a commercial or education Microsoft 365 tenant with Intune (or a qualifying M365 suite) already in use prior to mid-2026, now navigating this bundling change either reactively (a client asks "why do we suddenly have this") or proactively (an MSP auditing licensing spend before a renewal).

## How It Works

<details><summary>Full architecture</summary>

**The pre-2026 model.** Intune Suite was a standalone, per-user add-on (~$10/user/month at list price) layered on top of a qualifying base Intune license, bundling: Remote Help, Advanced Analytics, Endpoint Privilege Management (EPM), Enterprise Application Management (EAM), Microsoft Cloud PKI, Intune Plan 2 capabilities (Microsoft Tunnel for MAM, firmware-over-the-air updates for specialty devices, specialty device management for AR/VR and meeting-room hardware), and Intune Advanced Analytics/STIG-related capability in government clouds. Each capability was also separately purchasable as a standalone SKU for tenants who wanted only one piece.

**The 2026 change.** Microsoft's stated rationale (echoed across its own Intune Blog post and independent analyst commentary) was that price was the primary adoption blocker for Intune Suite — many organizations wanted one or two of its capabilities but balked at the full per-user add-on cost. Rather than continuing to sell it purely as a bolt-on, Microsoft split the bundle along its base-license tiers:

- **Microsoft 365 E3 / EMS E3-qualifying tenants** gain: Remote Help, Advanced Analytics, and the Intune Plan 2-tier capabilities (Microsoft Tunnel for MAM, FOTA updates, specialty device management).
- **Microsoft 365 E5-qualifying tenants** gain everything in the E3 tier **plus**: Endpoint Privilege Management (EPM), Enterprise Application Management (EAM), and Microsoft Cloud PKI.

This is a **tier-gated, not all-or-nothing, inclusion** — an E3 tenant does not get EPM/EAM/Cloud PKI for free; those remain either an E5 upgrade or a standalone add-on purchase. This asymmetry is the single highest-value fact for MSP client conversations: "we're on Intune Suite already" clients on E3 base licensing will NOT see Cloud PKI/EPM/EAM become free even after this change goes live in their tenant.

**Provisioning mechanics.** Bundled service plans provision automatically into qualifying tenants — there is no admin toggle, purchase flow, or consent step. Microsoft's stated process is a 30-day advance notice via the Microsoft 365 admin center Message Center, followed by automatic activation on a rolling schedule across the July 1 – August 1, 2026 window. This means two tenants on an identical SKU can show different bundled-plan availability for a period of weeks purely based on where they sit in Microsoft's rollout queue — not a misconfiguration on either side.

**What this is NOT.** This bundling event is architecturally unrelated to: the separate Microsoft Entra Suite/Global Secure Access licensing consolidation (`EntraSuiteLicensing-A/B.md` — a different product family entirely, despite the similar "suite folded into a tier" pattern); the Multi Admin Approval Graph-API/app-auth enforcement expansion (`MultiAdminApproval-A/B.md` — an authorization-control change, not a licensing one); or any change to RBAC, Conditional Access, or deployment configuration for the bundled features themselves.

</details>

## Dependency Stack

```
Microsoft-side rollout (July 1 – Aug 1 2026, tenant-by-tenant, 30-day MC notice)
    → Tenant holds a qualifying base SKU (M365/EMS E3 or E5 family — commercial, education, or government variant)
        → Tenant-level bundled service plan provisioning complete for this tenant
            → Base E3/E5 license ASSIGNED to the specific user (bundling changes SKU contents, not assignment)
                → Service plan not suppressed by group-based-licensing exclusion rules
                    → Feature-specific technical prerequisites (independent of licensing):
                        - EPM: EPM agent + elevation policy assignment
                        - Remote Help: BOTH helper and sharer licensed, tenant-wide enable switch on
                        - Cloud PKI: E5 tier only; 3-CA-per-tenant cap; BYOCA signing loop if applicable
                        - EAM: Enterprise App Catalog content sync + app source configuration
```

## Symptom → Cause Map

| Symptom | Most Likely Cause | Check |
|---|---|---|
| "We have E5 but EPM/Cloud PKI/EAM still shows unlicensed" | Tenant hasn't reached its rollout slot yet, or user lacks the base license assignment | `Get-MgSubscribedSku` (tenant SKU) + `Get-MgUserLicenseDetail` (user assignment + service plans) |
| "We're on E3 and expected Cloud PKI/EPM/EAM to be free now" | Expectation mismatch — those three are E5-only under this bundling | Confirm tier in the Symptom → Cause row above; no fix needed, this is by design |
| "Are we still paying for an add-on we don't need anymore?" | Standalone Intune Suite/EPM/Cloud PKI/Remote Help SKU still assigned alongside a qualifying base SKU | `Get-MgSubscribedSku` filtered for standalone add-on SKU patterns; escalate to licensing/procurement, not a technical fix |
| "Feature worked yesterday, today it's asking to buy Intune Suite again" | Extremely unlikely from this change (bundling doesn't get revoked) — check for a genuinely separate license expiration/removal event instead | `Get-MgUserLicenseDetail` history / M365 admin center billing history; do not assume this bundling caused a regression |
| "One user has the feature, an identical peer on the same SKU doesn't" | Group-based licensing service-plan exclusion difference between the two users, not a rollout gap | Compare `ServicePlans` disabled-plan lists between the two users |
| Client asks whether to cancel their standalone Intune Suite renewal | Depends entirely on their base SKU tier and per-feature needs | Walk through the E3-vs-E5 feature split table before recommending cancellation |

## Validation Steps

1. **Confirm the tenant's base SKU family and provisioning date.**
   ```powershell
   Get-MgSubscribedSku | Select-Object SkuPartNumber, SkuId, ConsumedUnits
   ```
   Good output: a recognizable `SPE_E3`/`SPE_E5`-family (or qualifying EMS/education/government equivalent) SkuPartNumber. Bad/inconclusive: an SKU family that doesn't map to any published qualifying list — treat as non-qualifying until confirmed otherwise via Microsoft's own current documentation, not a community blog.

2. **Confirm per-user assignment and service-plan enablement.**
   ```powershell
   Get-MgUserLicenseDetail -UserId "<UPN>" | Select-Object SkuPartNumber, ServicePlans
   ```
   Good output: the base SKU listed with the expected bundled service plan(s) present and `ProvisioningStatus = Success`. Bad output: SKU present but the specific bundled plan missing entirely (rollout not yet reached this tenant) or present but `Disabled` (group-based licensing exclusion).

3. **Confirm this is genuinely a licensing question and not a feature-specific misconfiguration.**
   Attempt the feature's own first validation step from its dedicated runbook (e.g., `CloudPKI-B.md` Triage step 4, `EPM-B.md` Triage step 4) — if that step also fails with a licensing-specific error, the issue is upstream in this runbook; if it fails for a different reason, licensing is fine and this runbook does not apply.

4. **Check for redundant standalone add-on spend.**
   ```powershell
   Get-MgSubscribedSku | Where-Object { $_.SkuPartNumber -match 'INTUNE_SUITE|EPM|CLOUD_PKI|ENTERPRISE_APP|REMOTE_HELP|ADVANCED_ANALYTICS' }
   ```
   Any result with `ConsumedUnits -gt 0` alongside a qualifying base SKU is a genuine review candidate, not an automatic cancellation.

## Troubleshooting Steps (by phase)

**Phase 1 — Tenant eligibility.** Confirm the base SKU is a genuinely qualifying variant. Grandfathered, regional, or unusual commercial agreement SKUs may not be covered identically to standard commercial E3/E5 — verify against Microsoft's current published eligibility list per tenant rather than assuming parity with a "standard" E3/E5 tenant.

**Phase 2 — Rollout timing.** If eligibility is confirmed but bundled plans are absent, check the tenant's Message Center for the specific activation notification and date. Before that date, absence is expected, not a fault.

**Phase 3 — Per-user assignment.** The most common single point of failure post-rollout: the tenant-wide entitlement exists, but the specific user was never assigned the base E3/E5 license, or was assigned via a group-based licensing policy that has this specific service plan disabled.

**Phase 4 — Redundant spend review.** Once technical eligibility is fully confirmed, separately evaluate whether any standalone add-on SKUs the tenant holds are now partially or fully redundant. This is a billing conversation with the account owner, not something to resolve unilaterally by removing licenses — removing a standalone SKU that's still covering E3-tier users' access to an E5-only capability (if the client hasn't upgraded to E5) would cause a real outage.

**Phase 5 — Handoff.** Once licensing is confirmed correct end-to-end and the feature still doesn't work as expected, hand off entirely to that feature's own dedicated runbook — this topic's scope ends at "the license and service plan are present and enabled."

## Remediation Playbooks

<details><summary>Playbook 1 — Full tenant licensing audit ahead of a Intune Suite renewal decision</summary>

1. Inventory all base SKUs (`Get-MgSubscribedSku`) and classify each as E3-qualifying, E5-qualifying, or neither.
2. Inventory all standalone Intune Suite/EPM/Cloud PKI/Remote Help/EAM/Advanced Analytics SKUs and their consumed-seat counts.
3. Cross-reference: for each user consuming a standalone add-on seat, check whether their assigned base SKU already grants that same capability under this bundling.
4. Produce a per-user reconciliation table: base SKU tier | bundled capability already covered | standalone add-on still held | recommended action (keep / cancel / upgrade to E5).
5. Present findings to the account/licensing owner — this is an advisory deliverable, not something the audit script itself should act on. Do not cancel or reassign licenses without explicit approval, consistent with this repo's standing practice around any procurement/billing action.

</details>

<details><summary>Playbook 2 — Tenant approaching its rollout window but bundled plans not yet visible</summary>

1. Confirm the base SKU is genuinely qualifying (Phase 1 above).
2. Search Message Center for the specific rollout notification; note its stated activation date.
3. If before the stated date: no action needed, expected behavior — communicate the timeline to the client rather than escalating.
4. If after the stated date and still absent: this is the one scenario in this topic that is a genuine Microsoft-side gap, not a client-side misconfiguration — open a Microsoft support case referencing the specific Message Center post ID rather than continuing to self-diagnose. There is no documented admin-facing action to force re-provisioning.

Rollback: not applicable — this playbook is entirely diagnostic/escalation, no configuration change is made.

</details>

## Command Cheat Sheet

```powershell
# Tenant-wide SKU inventory
Get-MgSubscribedSku | Select-Object SkuPartNumber, SkuId, ConsumedUnits

# Filter for standalone add-on SKUs (potential redundant spend)
Get-MgSubscribedSku | Where-Object { $_.SkuPartNumber -match 'INTUNE_SUITE|EPM|CLOUD_PKI|ENTERPRISE_APP|REMOTE_HELP|ADVANCED_ANALYTICS' }

# Per-user license + service plan detail
Get-MgUserLicenseDetail -UserId "<UPN>" | Select-Object SkuPartNumber, ServicePlans

# Assign a base E3/E5 license to a user
$sku = Get-MgSubscribedSku | Where-Object SkuPartNumber -eq "<SkuPartNumber>"
Set-MgUserLicense -UserId "<UPN>" -AddLicenses @{ SkuId = $sku.SkuId } -RemoveLicenses @()

# Group-based licensing: check for service-plan exclusions on a group
Get-MgGroup -GroupId "<GroupId>" -Property "assignedLicenses" | Select-Object -ExpandProperty AssignedLicenses

# Bulk export for a reconciliation review
Get-MgUser -All | Select-Object UserPrincipalName, @{N='Skus';E={(Get-MgUserLicenseDetail -UserId $_.Id).SkuPartNumber -join ';'}} |
    Export-Csv -Path "IntuneSuiteLicensingReview.csv" -NoTypeInformation
```

## 🎓 Learning Pointers

- **The E3/E5 split is asymmetric by design, not a rollout artifact.** Cloud PKI, EPM, and EAM are permanently E5-gated under this model — don't expect them to arrive on E3 tenants in a "later wave." [Advanced Microsoft Intune capabilities now available in Microsoft 365 E3 and E5](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftintuneblog/advanced-microsoft-intune-capabilities-now-available-in-microsoft-365-e3-and-e5/4529335)

- **This is a rolling, tenant-by-tenant provisioning event with a real time window (July 1 – Aug 1 2026), not an instant global flip.** Two seemingly identical tenants can legitimately be in different states for weeks — verify Message Center before treating absence as a bug.

- **Redundant-spend review is a genuinely valuable proactive MSP engagement created by this change** — clients who bought Intune Suite specifically for Remote Help/Advanced Analytics/Plan 2 features on an E3 base may now be paying twice; this is worth raising unprompted at the next licensing review rather than waiting for the client to notice.

- **Cross-reference `CloudPKI-A/B.md`, `EPM-A/B.md`, `RemoteHelp-A/B.md`, and `EnterpriseAppManagement-A/B.md`** — each of those runbooks' own Learning Pointers/licensing-check steps should be read alongside this one; this topic intentionally does not duplicate their feature-specific technical detail.

- **Source discrepancies exist across secondary/community commentary on this topic** (list-price change amounts, a minority-reported alternate rollout-completion date) that could not be resolved to a single authoritative figure via the sources available at time of writing — treat the E3/E5 feature-tier split and the July–August 2026 window as the confirmed core, and verify any dollar figure directly against the tenant's own billing/admin center rather than quoting a community number to a client.
